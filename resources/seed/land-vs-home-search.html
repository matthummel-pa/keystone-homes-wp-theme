If they are shopping homes

Lead with type, price, beds and baths. Acreage is secondary unless they want hobby space, a barn, or room for a shop. On a rural search, commute and well-or-public-water still belong on the card — not buried in the description.

  • Payment estimate next to the photo
  • School and commute notes on the area pages
  • Evening and weekend showing slots

If they are shopping land

Lead with acreage, access, utilities and buildability. Price-per-acre helps scanning. A parcel without a perc answer or a legal driveway is not the same product as a turnkey farmhouse — do not mix those in one default filter set.

  • Perc and septic status called out early
  • Road frontage and driveway notes
  • Area context — zoning changes from Oak Hollow to Border Farms

Shared tools that help both

House shoppers and acreage buyers still need the same next step: a scannable inventory, a way to save a search, and one showing request.

  • Map and grid toggle
  • Saved searches and alerts
  • One booking flow for tours
  • Short FAQ with schema markup

What to publish next

Write separate notes for “homes for sale” intent and “land for sale” intent, then link both to your listings filters and the showing scheduler. That is how a realtor site ranks for both shoppers without stuffing one page.
